# CT-1 result: proxied response header blocklist

**Result: done.** `RESPONSE_HEADER_BLOCKLIST` (`packages/shared/src/fetch.ts`) gains `clear-site-data`, `content-security-policy`, `content-security-policy-report-only`, `refresh`, `report-to`, `reporting-endpoints` and `nel`, appended after the existing 11 names (18 in total). Egress and the edge gateway both build their sets from this list at module load, so the one edit reaches both hops; neither `proxy.ts` nor `gateway/fetch.ts` changed. `link`, `www-authenticate`, `etag`, `retry-after` and the rate-limit headers still pass.

`link` stays off the list on purpose. Browsers do not act on a `Link` header in a `fetch()` response, and stripping it would break `rel="next"` pagination (decision recorded 2026-10-09 in `decisions.md`).

## Coordinator correction

The docblock text came from the task document and said the policy headers are "inert on a fetch() response". That is wrong for the two most dangerous ones. Browsers apply `Clear-Site-Data` and `NEL` from any response of the origin, including `fetch()` responses, and the gateway's response is same-origin with the app. The comment now says that, and that the CSP, `Refresh` and reporting headers apply if the response is opened as a document.
